# Editing style

Read before planning a reel. These are starting preferences, not a formula
or a claim that a particular edit will increase reach. The current brief
and the creator's feedback take precedence.

## Voice and presence

Preserve an energetic, curious, conversational delivery. Keep useful
explanations, expressive gestures, smiles, and moments of discovery. Choose
the take that communicates best, not automatically the last or most polished
take. Match the creator's demonstrated experience; do not manufacture
expertise or results.

Exclamation marks and small, relevant emojis can support the speaker's
expression. Do not equate enthusiasm with giant reaction graphics, forced
slang, or constant motion. Only claim personal experience the creator has
confirmed. Fictional template material must never become their testimony.

## Default edit

- Give each short video one useful takeaway. Follow the requested length
  rather than imposing a fixed duration on every topic.
- Remove false starts, duplication, and empty waiting. Preserve breathing,
  a meaningful pause after a question, and natural sentence continuity.
- Start with two or three purposeful framing changes for a one-minute
  reel, adjusting to the recording. No zoom timer or tap on every cut.
- Use readable phrase captions in one or two lines. Highlight selected
  important words; do not bounce or recolor every spoken word. Check each
  language at phone size, especially Chinese and mixed-language phrases.
- Start with warm white (#FFF8EC), charcoal backing where needed, and one
  yellow accent (#FFD166). This is an initial palette, not a fixed brand.
- Show a quote, number, screenshot, or diagram only when it helps explain
  the spoken idea. Use one explanatory visual at a time, with enough time
  to read it and breathing room between visuals. Protect face and gestures.
- Memes are off by default. A small emoji can support a real expression;
  there is no permanent reaction slot or emoji quota. Add a meme only when
  specifically requested or included in an approved outline.
- Prioritize clear voice. No automatic sound effects on cuts, captions, or
  cards. An occasional quiet effect or music bed is optional; confirm the
  track's usage rights for the actual distribution.
- End on the spoken thought, expression, or relevant invitation. A frozen
  promotional end card is optional, not the default.

## Adapt the delivery

These are editorial starting points to test, not platform algorithm rules.
Preserve the underlying claim and the creator's voice across versions.

| Destination | Starting adaptation |
|---|---|
| X | Lead with a specific insight or question; let the accompanying post supply context. Make the video understandable muted. |
| Xiaohongshu / 小红书 / RedNote | Use natural Chinese when requested, a clear practical promise, and a readable takeaway viewers can save. Compose a separate cover rather than covering the opening with a title. |
| TikTok | Enter the conversation immediately; preserve expressive delivery and a quick concrete payoff. Trends are optional. |
| YouTube Shorts | Make the opening understandable without a caption or prior episode. Deliver the promised explanation and finish cleanly. |

For each destination, check the current posting preview and UI overlap.
Do not assume Instagram's coordinates fit every platform. See layout.md.

## Outline and preview

Before editing a new video, propose its takeaway, opening, approximate
length, intended destination, selected visual beats, and any departures
from these defaults. If that outline is already approved, proceed without
asking again. Preparing a transcript/contact sheet is part of planning.

After outline approval, make a representative 10–15 second preview with
captions and at least one explanatory visual. Let the creator review that
before completing the full reel, unless they have already authorized the full edit
or waived the preview. Approval to update this skill does not approve a
particular recording's edit or publication.
